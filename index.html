About Kwa Gogo

At grandmother's house

Kwa Gogo translates to ‘at grandmother’s house.’

The house was named by her grandmother, at whose house they often went for rest. Whether it was during school holidays as an offering of child care for her parents, or the end of year holidays or mid year celebrations, it was a house where time seemingly slowed down; where she ate from the land, paid attention to the offerings from the earth, lived simply yet felt held.

This is for those who need a grandmother’s offering of space. Now with even more disconnection and dispossession from the land, this space invites us to the generous spirit of Gogo’s third space.

“It was a ‘come as you are’ party, and all y’all come.”

If you brought something, you shared it. If you didn’t, someone shared with you.

Maya Angelou, paraphrased from Gather Together in My Name

About the Residency

A sanctuary. A refusal.
An experiment.

This is a living experiment, conceived to offer a space as an offering.

The residency is a two-week long sanctuary held at a private home in Mnarani, area in Kilifi. Set at Mnarani creek with a glimpse of the Indian Ocean and among local residents, the house offers a simple and grounding environment with shared and individual spaces of rest.

It is an invitation into a third space to find respite. It is a symbolic act of refusal of a dominant and extractive system that requires and only values our doing within the lens of production of capitalism, i.e. we’re only valuable if we produce or provide goods and services to earn money or at a profit.

This space is an act of our own remembrance of welcoming visitors and travellers who needed a place to rest their bodies. It is inspired by the spirit of our grandmothers who often played a role in welcoming many into their homes.

“How do you find rest in a capitalist, white supremacist, patriarchal, ableist system?”

Her answer: tap into something playful and unruly in yourself. Learn to slip away, like an escape artist.

The question, from Tricia Hersey's We will Rest! The art of escape

It is a practice of our collective survival, to reclaim time to listen and remember our connection to spirit, our ancestors, our inner wisdom, our sovereignty and our inherent value as human beings.

Participants are invited to contribute to an anthology of an escape to grandmother’s house in either text, audio or visual format in any language of their choice.

What kind of support is available at the residency

The space has been created for you to slow down and rest with no formal facilitation in place. During the course of your stay you will have access to choose from 2-3 in-person offerings by Afrikan wellbeing practitioners based in or near Kilifi.

Eligibility

Who the house is open to

The space is offered to women in the diversity from the Global South* including the South* of the North. We define South* as any of the Global Majority countries and South* of the North as anyone at the margins in Western countries either as migrants, queer women in their diversity.

  • If you offer intergenerational care-giving including as a first born sibling.
  • Care-givers of elderly or chronically ill family.
  • Chronically ill / those managing chronic pain.
  • Domestic workers.
  • Queer women in their diversity.
  • Artists or cultural workers whose work disrupts dominant extractive systems and challenges oppression.

We are able to offer modest transport bursary and honorarium to those in need pending availability of resources. Please indicate in your application.

We also welcome you to apply on behalf of those who might not have access.

Details of Residency

What the days make space for

  • Simple vegetarian meals are cooked four times a week using local and fresh ingredients; otherwise ready to cook meals available in the fridge/pantry. Please share any allergy or dietary needs in your application so we can accommodate.
  • Two bedroom ensuite, shared living room space and kitchen, up-to 2 fellows at a time.
  • Residents are expected to cover additional personal living expenses and any other expenses unrelated to the residency.

Note on accessibility

We are committed to creating a space that is as accessible and affirming as possible. However, we acknowledge that accessibility can look different depending on local infrastructure and resources. The house is mostly accessible, generally quiet, and partially free of stairs, but we cannot guarantee full accessibility in all areas. Please let us know your needs in your application, and we will do our best to accommodate you.

Please note that Kilifi is a rural town whose infrastructure is in flux. The inner roads to access the property might be affected with heavy rainfall, however just as the locals, with the right shoes you should be fine. We also experience occasional power cuts, in such instances we have solar powered lights.

Sustaining the House

Resourced through friendship

The house is resourced through and in friendship. It is an active space of love, generosity, relational care through sharing and redistributing of resources.

We live in the home seasonally to make space for the residency.

There are real costs of sustaining the place (Wifi, Electricity, Water, Housekeeping and Gardening), and while we don’t ask our guests to pay for a ‘real cost’ of the stay, we ask for a monetary contribution through a sliding scale model that allows us to continue to sustain the home.

Sliding Scale note from @from the roots

A sliding scale allows for differently resourced people to participate and is a practice of economic justice.

Higher costs represent a contribution from a comfortable-income individual, and one which supports our community and our being able to offer lower costs through distributing wealth.

You are invited to give as much as would not generate hardship and consider both your needs and ours.

Questions

Before you decide

How long is the residency?

We acknowledge that time is a flexible resource, as such applicants are welcome to request for a minimum 7 days or maximum 3 weeks residency. The residency is conceived as a two-week long sanctuary.

What does the stay include?

Two bedroom ensuite, shared living room space and kitchen, up-to 2 fellows at a time. Simple vegetarian meals are cooked four times a week using local and fresh ingredients; otherwise ready to cook meals are available in the fridge/pantry.

Is there facilitation or a programme?

The space has been created for you to slow down and rest with no formal facilitation in place. During the course of your stay you will have access to choose from 2-3 in-person offerings by Afrikan wellbeing practitioners based in or near Kilifi.

How do I get there?

There are no delivery/taxi hailing apps in Kilifi. We rely on service providers who know the place. Please ask your contact, Daisy/Kwem, to share this with you. Equally, there is no pinned map of the location. We revert back to the old ways of arriving at a location, asking around for directions :) For your convenience, a rider’s number has been provided by your contact (Kwem/Daisy) to direct you. You will pay them between Ksh 100-200.

What if I can't afford the full scale?

You are invited to give as much as would not generate hardship and consider both your needs and ours. We are able to offer modest transport bursary and honorarium to those in need pending availability of resources. Please indicate in your application. It is possible to split your payments into 2 tranches. Let us know if you need this in the form.

How are applications decided?

Submissions will be evaluated solely on the content of the application and the decision by the evaluation does in no way offer judgement on the perceived quality of an applicant’s work.

What happens during my stay?

Clean up after yourself often: please do not leave food/drinks unattended on surfaces and in the sink for long as it would bring bugs etc. Should you request cleaning services during your stay, we have our amazing and trusted Rehema who will be happy to assist. Rehema’s contact is 0746 033 249. A minimum fee of KES 1,000 to be paid directly to her for each day she may be required. She is one of our cherished team members, and it’s important to us that she feels just as valued and appreciated by our guests. Please note that Kilifi is not a fast economy, things may happen slowly and against the pace that you are used to; we invite you to settle into this discomfort.

What happens after my stay?

Please make use of the sage available on the kitchen counter to smoke all the rooms. Make sure that the windows have been closed. Share with us any feedback or thoughts about this offering of mutual aid for vacation. We invite you to think of the ways in which you may be able to make space as an offering in your life.
